\subsection{Electron Energy Calibration}\label{sec:ELECTRON_CALIBRATION}
Even after lifetime and charge calibration, small systematic biases may remain in the reconstructed electron energy due to recombination modelling, imperfect response, or clustering inefficiencies. We apply a linear correction to account for this residual offset:

\begin{align}
E^\text{cluster}_{e} = \alpha\left(E^{\text{true}}_e,\,\overrightarrow{p}\,,n_\text{hit}\right) \cdot E^\text{true}_{e} &+ \beta\left(E^{\text{true}}_e,\,\overrightarrow{p}\,,n_\text{hit}\right)
\label{eq:ELECTRON_COMPUTATION}
\end{align}

Here, \( E^\text{cluster}_{e} \) is the computed electron energy for the cluster, \( E^\text{true}_{e} \) is the true energy of the main electron, and \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) are the linear correction parameters that depend on the true energy, the vertex of the electron's interaction (represented by \( \overrightarrow{p} \)), and the number of hits in the resulting cluster (\( n_\text{hit} \)). These parameters are determined by fitting the distribution of \( E^\text{cluster}_{e} \) versus \( E^\text{true}_{e} \) for the main clusters across the dataset.

There is no significant difference between the \gls{hd} and \gls{vd} geometries in terms of these calibration parameters.

The final calibrated electron energy, \( E^\text{reco}_e \), is obtained by applying the inverse of the linear correction to the cluster energy, effectively removing the bias and improving the agreement between reconstructed and true energies, as illustrated in \cref{fig:CHARGE_CALIBRATION}.

\begin{align}
E^\text{reco}_e &= \dfrac{E^\text{cluster}_e - b(n_\text{hit})}{a(n_\text{hit})} \\
\text{with } \quad \{a(n_\text{hit}),\,b(n_\text{hit})\} &= \mathcal{A}\left[\alpha\left(E^{\text{true}}_e,\,\overrightarrow{p}\,,n_\text{hit}\right),\,\beta\left(E^{\text{true}}_e,\,\overrightarrow{p}\,,n_\text{hit}\right)\right]\,. \nonumber
\label{eq:ELECTRON_CALIBRATION}
\end{align}

\Cref{eq:ELECTRON_CALIBRATION} shows the final step of the electron energy calibration, where \( \mathcal{A} \) represents the fitting procedure used to determine the parameters \( a(n_\text{hit}) \) and \( b(n_\text{hit}) \) from the distributions of \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) as functions of the number of hits in the cluster. This calibration ensures that the reconstructed electron energy is as close as possible to the true energy, accounting for any systematic biases that may arise from detector effects or reconstruction inefficiencies.
